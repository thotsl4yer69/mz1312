THE INVESTIGATION DESK. Foundation repair two. CPPINV3027, developing investigation plans.

The national standard requires three plans of specified types, not three arbitrary examples. Plan one is a factual general insurance or financial crime investigation. Plan two is a factual workers compensation or internal discipline investigation. Plan three is a surveillance investigation. A location search and two unrelated background checks are not the required combination.

For a general insurance exercise, imagine an insurer reporting that a gallery display was damaged overnight. The initial complaint names a visitor, but an access log is incomplete and a photograph was taken the next day. A proper plan asks how the damage happened, which existing records may answer the question, who has direct knowledge, when recordings could be overwritten and what evidence is outside scope. It does not begin with prove the visitor damaged it. Document the authorised brief, enquiries, resources, deadlines, expenditure approvals, report recipients and safe evidence preservation.

For the second factual plan, imagine a workers compensation incident involving a reported lifting injury. Relevant material includes rosters, training records, a supervisor account and a worker interview that may need an interpreter. The investigation must identify the specific incident, actual work activities and source limitations. It must not purport to decide medical causation without suitable expertise. Plan procedural fairness, participant needs, privacy, appropriate contact and the handling of conflicting records. If you choose an internal discipline inquiry instead, identify its particular employment policy and fairness process.

The third is a surveillance plan. Use a clearly authorised or assessor-approved training setting involving consenting participants rather than assuming that surveillance powers follow from a client's instructions. Define the purpose, subject identification, permitted site and observation positions, recording controls, equipment checks, communication, clock synchronisation, safety, fatigue management, unplanned contact, loss of view and stopping conditions. Explain when the operation must be changed or abandoned because of unsafe or unlawful conditions.

A useful shared planning structure has eight parts. Start with authority and instructions. State objectives, scope and exclusions. List known and missing information. Link methods to sources and alternative explanations. Allocate staff, equipment and budget. Identify safety, privacy and other risks with contingencies. Sequence tasks with deadlines and review checkpoints. Finish with plan approval, secure records and reporting arrangements. The Academy may prescribe its own template; use the actual assessment requirements when producing the final portfolio.

Test the plan against change. If the gallery footage does not show the relevant doorway, or a critical interviewee becomes unavailable, record the new information and review every dependent task. Who approved the variation? Which plan version was superseded? What new risk or cost applies? Preserve earlier versions rather than silently replacing them.

For assessment, keep three distinct approved case briefs and three personally prepared plans, each containing the correct required investigation category and appropriate reviews. A plan is a justified proposal for work; a report describes work actually completed. Never claim you conducted a surveillance operation just because its plan is written.
