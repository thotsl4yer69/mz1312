THE INVESTIGATION DESK. Foundation repair three. CPPINV3028, investigating and locating subjects.

The national performance requirement concerns three different subjects. One ongoing fictional Alex Morgan case, however carefully explained, is a teaching example rather than the whole practical portfolio. The assessor determines the acceptable genuine or workplace-realistic simulations and the evidence required to demonstrate successful locating.

Begin each locate enquiry with four questions. Who instructed you? What legitimate decision or communication is sought? How do you identify the intended subject? And what could happen if the wrong person is contacted or their location is disclosed? A client being confident does not remove the need to verify identity or lawful purpose. Distinguish finding a current address from arranging a lawful forwarding contact; they can meet different aims and must be reported accurately.

Use three separate training cases to rehearse the method. First, an organisation seeks to forward an invitation to a former volunteer through an authorised contact. Second, an approved legal-process simulation has a correctly identified subject but an outdated address. Third, a name search identifies two individuals with similar names and conflicting histories. Keep these as clearly labelled training scenarios and use Academy-authorised sources if actual performance is required.

Each case needs a current work brief, an identity profile with source attribution and a lawful research plan. Search engines, public registers, business records and social profiles can all help, but they provide different kinds of evidence. A business register can identify a listed office; it does not prove a person lives there. Three websites copying one old directory are not three independent confirmations. A profile image does not, by itself, prove identity. Note when a claim was published and whether its source may be outdated.

Publicly accessible is not the same as unrestricted use. Electoral-roll access and reproduction have particular conditions. Commercial databases have permissible-use terms. Accessing leaked credentials, impersonating a professional, inventing a delivery pretext or bypassing a closed social account is not made lawful merely by calling the investigation open-source intelligence.

If direct enquiry is authorised, make contact honestly, use narrow questions and record who supplied the answer, when, how, and whether they had first-hand knowledge. For an approved field visit, plan a safe location, check-in procedure, appropriate access and exit. An uncooperative person is not automatically deceptive. A refusal and the reason given, if any, are themselves outcomes to record without unfair inference.

Each activity entry should identify date, time, source, method, authority, result, limitation and next decision. Preserve contradictory evidence. If two candidates share a name, do not choose one because they match the client's theory. If no current location can be established, say so rather than turning a possible lead into a confirmed outcome.

Finally prepare three separate reports, distinguishing the subject's identity, the verified contact or location, source quality, lawful restrictions, unresolved matters and the client's next permitted choice. Ask the assessor what level of locating outcome and simulated conduct is required. Correctly reporting a failed lead is professional; whether it meets this unit's complete performance evidence is an assessor decision.
