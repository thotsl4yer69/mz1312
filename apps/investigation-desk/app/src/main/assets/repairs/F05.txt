THE INVESTIGATION DESK. Foundation repair five. CPPSEC3124, preparing and presenting evidence in court.

This is the fifth foundation unit even though its original lectures are numbered thirty-six to thirty-nine. The national unit expects preparation and presentation of evidence in two separate court proceedings, including direct observation of the required performance in an appropriate workplace or realistic environment. The lesson you are hearing cannot substitute for that observation.

The Academy's study material also includes a four-part Giving Evidence classroom video series of about twenty-five minutes, courtroom procedures and forms. Watch and use those original resources. If a video or required form is not accessible, obtain the proper source or assessor guidance. It is not appropriate to invent the contents or claim the video was watched.

For the first simulated proceeding, use a fictional exhibition-centre incident. A security officer sees somebody move a barrier, then hears a crash. The officer does not see the moment of impact. CCTV starts after the first movement. A witness repeats something another person said. A maintenance log has an unclear author. Identify the precise propositions each source may support. Keep direct observation, recorded account, inference and expert opinion separate.

Prepare a brief that a legal representative or assessor can check. Include the original case instruction, chronology, witness notes, relevant statements, exhibits, source identifiers, copies and conversions, and custody trail. Do not conceal a broken exhibit seal or missing acknowledgement. Explain the gap and seek guidance. An exhibit register records what happened; it is not a guarantee that a court must admit the material. Legal rules about hearsay, authenticity, privilege and opinion depend on context and purpose.

Attend the appropriate briefing and confirm the hearing's jurisdiction and practical requirements, your role, scheduled time, who calls you, permitted aids and recording rules. Test any video playback in advance. Reconcile transferred exhibits with an actual recipient acknowledgement. Never assume a form is signed because it includes a signature box.

During evidence, listen to each question. If asked whether you saw the subject break the barrier and you only saw them move it, explain exactly that limitation. If a question is ambiguous or compound, seek clarification. If you need notes to refresh memory, follow the hearing's directions and explain when the notes were made. Correct mistakes openly. Do not guess about a time, distance or opinion outside your competence just to sound confident. The court controls the procedure.

For the second proceeding, use a separate file: a shop-entrance incident with inconsistent camera time and an uncertain cleaning record. Prepare its own witness brief and exhibit records. Two cover pages attached to the same evidence do not necessarily demonstrate two independent performances. Direct observed questioning, including examination and cross-examination as applicable, must be genuine or the assessor's authorised simulation.

After each court performance, record what actually occurred, what exhibits were retained, any corrections, directions about evidence and secure storage. Distinguish the simulated hearing's outcome from the assessor's competency decision. A favourable courtroom result is not automatically a pass.

Your checkpoint is simple: two proceedings, two independent source files, two properly prepared witness roles, observed evidence-giving, documented exhibit handling and the original Academy materials. The five foundation units lead toward qualification; listening to them does not itself award one.
