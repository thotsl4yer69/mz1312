THE INVESTIGATION DESK. Foundation repair four. CPPINV3029, quality investigative services to clients.

This unit is more than friendly communication. The national performance requirement specifies services provided to three different clients across workers compensation, general insurance and compulsory third party liability. These must be distinct matters. One fictional client whose story changes across episodes is not a demonstration that you personally serviced three separate clients.

For the first training case, imagine a workers compensation insurer instructing a factual enquiry into an alleged lifting event. Determine the issue actually referred, the client contact and approved source channels. Plan fair participation by relevant workers, including access, language, scheduling and consent where applicable. Keep medical causation and claim decisions outside the investigator's unsupported expertise. Communicate what is completed and what remains unresolved.

For general insurance, imagine a property-damage claim with conflicting photographs, a missing inspection register and a Friday deadline. The client asks for a final finding. A professional response states which records are verified, what their limitations are, how the missing information affects the conclusion, and whether an interim report or authorised extension is warranted. An investigation may provide a sound report even when the client's suspicion remains unproved.

The third client is a solicitor conducting a compulsory third party liability inquiry following a motor vehicle incident. Establish the correct state scheme, the actual question in the instructions and the proposed use of the findings. A photograph of damage does not independently prove collision speed, fault or injury causation. Identify original observations, reliable records, corroboration and questions for specialist or legal advice.

For all three matters, keep an intake sheet with client identity, authorised contact, purpose, scope, excluded work, budget, times, reporting method, confidentiality and approvals. Agree how variations occur. Keep clear progress updates that separate acts already performed from suggested future enquiries. Seek reasonable adjustments when a witness requires accessibility support. Do not sacrifice safety or fairness to meet an arbitrary deadline.

Complaints need independent analysis. A client may be unhappy with an outcome, have a legitimate complaint about delay and identify an incorrect invoice on the same call. Check each issue against the agreement and actual records. Correct a billing or administrative mistake, explain the evidentiary result clearly and record action taken. Seek feedback about service clarity, communication and timing rather than count only compliments.

A reliable handover contains the current and previous brief, work and cost approvals, contact record, source register, evidence item identifiers, access restrictions, progress messages, final report, outstanding questions and closure/retention decision. Keep private or sensitive information available only to authorised roles.

The practical portfolio consists of three independently identifiable client-service files of the specified case types, with genuine or approved simulated acts and the assessor's required evidence. Rehearsed scripts are training. They do not become records of actual calls or approvals because they are written in the first person.

Ask yourself: can another authorised investigator identify exactly what each client wanted, what was actually delivered, what remains uncertain and who approved each variation? If not, there is more quality work to do.
