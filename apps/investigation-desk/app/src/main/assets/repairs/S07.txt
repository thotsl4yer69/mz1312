THE INVESTIGATION DESK. Practical clinic: surveillance operations, safe vehicles and reporting.

CPPINV3033, CPPINV3034 and CPPINV3035 belong together as a workstream, but each tests a different competence. A surveillance plan is not evidence of surveillance completed. A vehicle inspection is not evidence that a safe operation occurred. A tidy report is not proof the underlying observations exist.

For CPPINV3033, the national performance evidence requires three different surveillance operations including specified vehicle-based and on-foot elements. It also requires practical observation records, subject descriptions, choices of observation positions, personal endurance practices, and responses to contingencies such as unexpected contact or possible anti-surveillance indicators. Practice only in legal, assessor-approved environments with the required authorisation. A client instruction is not blanket permission to trespass, record prohibited activities or follow an unsuspecting private person in a training exercise.

Before observation, confirm purpose, identity, scope, location, permissions, equipment suitability, check-in contacts, withdrawal triggers, emergency procedure and reporting expectations. During work, record the start and end time, reference clock, location, source of identification, directly observed actions, changes of position and every significant break in view. A camera image does not show what happened outside the frame. Two observers may have different vantage points, and their accounts should not be merged as if they saw identical events.

CPPINV3034 requires appropriate vehicle selection and operation across three different surveillance activities. Check roadworthiness, tyres, maintenance, registration, safe parking, storage of equipment, communications and fatigue management. Follow normal road rules. At a red traffic light, you stop even if the observation opportunity is lost. Record the resulting coverage gap. A lost view is not a reason to create a collision risk or drive beyond your authority.

CPPINV3035 concerns surveillance reporting, with three distinct reports drawn from the required covert-investigation case types. Each should be supported by actual daily observation sheets, source notes, appropriately sourced photographs, video receipts and a final itemised invoice. Show how the report's assertions trace to each original log entry or evidence item. Use precise observational verbs such as walked, lifted, carried and placed. Avoid unsupported interpretations such as fraudulently, painlessly or deliberately where evidence is inadequate.

The Academy's Richmond report exercise includes source notes with inconsistencies. Preserve them, explain what needs checking and avoid silently cleaning the chronology. The Delta Johnson exercise refers to activity footage. Without that original footage, you cannot accurately supply the subject's movements, walking distances, vehicle details or screenshots. A guide to writing a report is not evidence that particular activities happened.

For a practical portfolio, prepare separate source and observation records for each authorised operation, with genuinely performed work or assessor-approved simulation. Keep each method and vehicle requirement visible, and write the required reports from the records that actually exist. Do not simply relabel one training session as three independent investigations without confirming the national unit evidence and the assessor's instructions.

The test of quality is whether a reviewer can trace every significant report sentence back to the exact authorised observation, time and source, identify gaps and reproduce the costs. If not, the file remains incomplete.
